# Wildlife Tracking Species Parameter Reference

This is a compiled species parameter reference for the wildlife narrative generator. All numerical values are sourced from published telemetry studies, USFWS reports, state wildlife agency reports, and peer-reviewed ecology papers. Parameters marked **Not found** or **Not quantified** indicate gaps where published web-accessible sources did not provide clean numbers; see the "Honest Assessment of Gaps" section at the end.

---

## 1. Gray Wolf (Canis lupus) -- Northern Rockies / Yellowstone

### Weight by Sex
- **Males**: 70-145 lbs (32-66 kg); average ~110 lbs (50 kg) in Northern Rockies
- **Females**: 60-100 lbs (27-45 kg); females ~80-85% of male weight
- Monthly seasonal variation: **Not found** in published search results. No peer-reviewed source provided month-by-month weight curves for wolves.

### Daily Movement Distance
- Average: 10-20 miles/day (16-32 km/day) within territory
- Can cover 30+ miles/day (48+ km) when prey is scarce
- Spend ~35% of time traveling
- Seasonal breakdown (winter vs summer): **Not quantified** in search results, though movement is tied to prey (elk) migration patterns

### Home Range / Territory Size
- Yellowstone average: **165 sq mi (427 km2)** overall
- Another estimate: **891 km2 (344 sq mi)** average
- Northern range territories ~1/3 the size of interior territories
- General range: 30-2,500 sq mi depending on prey density
- Relationship to pack size: larger packs hold larger territories, but specific pack-size-to-area function **not quantified** in results

### Pack Size Distribution
- Yellowstone average: **10.7-11.8** wolves per pack
- Range: 4 to 25 per pack
- ~8-10 packs typically in Yellowstone; ~1 dissolves and 1 forms annually
- Distribution shape (how many of each size): **Not found** as a histogram

### Denning Dates
- Breeding: **February**
- Gestation: ~63 days
- Pup birth: **April** (mid-April typically)
- Birth weight: ~1 lb (0.45 kg)
- Eyes open: 10-15 days, weight ~4 lbs
- First standing/walking: 15-25 days
- First den emergence: **20-77 days** post-birth (May-June)
- Move to rendezvous sites: **8-10 weeks** post-birth (June-July)

### Dispersal
- **Source**: Jimenez et al. 2017, J. Wildlife Management (1,600+ radio-collared wolves, 1993-2008)
- ~10% of known population disperses annually
- Age: 37% yearlings, 59% adults, 4% pups
- Mean distance: males 98.1 km (61 mi), females 87.7 km (55 mi)
- 3.4% dispersed >300 km
- Sex ratio: 169M : 128F (males more likely to disperse)
- Reproduction after dispersal: 28% of dispersing males reproduced vs 42% of dispersing females

### Mortality Causes and Rates (Inside Yellowstone)
- **77% natural causes** inside park; **77% human-caused** outside park in GYE
- Natural cause breakdown (adult wolves):
  - Intraspecific conflict (wolves killing wolves): **42%**
  - Natural unknown: **15%**
  - Interspecific predation: **8%**
  - Malnutrition/starvation: **5%**
  - Other natural: **4%**
  - Disease: **3%**
- Human causes: **17%** (harvest 7%, vehicle 6%, illegal 2%, control 1%, other 1%)
- Unknown: **6%**
- Disease outbreaks: CDV outbreaks in 1999, 2005, 2008 reduced pup survival to ~28%
- Sarcoptic mange entered population January 2007, now endemic

### Seasonal Elevation Use
- Yellowstone northern range: 1,500-3,400 m elevation, with 87% of area between 1,500-2,400 m
- Wolves follow elk to lower elevations in winter
- Specific seasonal elevation values for wolves themselves: **Not separately quantified** from elk

### GPS Collar Fix Success Rate
- General GPS collar fix rates: 87-99% depending on environment and interval
- In open terrain (wolf habitat): typically **92-99%**
- Forest canopy and terrain significantly reduce rates
- Yellowstone-specific wolf collar fix rate: **Not found** as a published number

### Litter Size
- Average at den emergence: **4.4 pups** (another source: 4.7)
- Range: 1-9 pups (typically 4-6)

### Pup Survival
- Average surviving to late December: **3.1-3.2 pups** (of 4.4 at emergence)
- ~65-70% survival from emergence to end of year
- 2022 data: 40 of 58 pups survived (69%) to year-end
- During CDV outbreaks: pup survival drops to ~28%

**Key Sources:**

## 2. Elk (Cervus canadensis) -- Yellowstone / Northern Rockies

### Weight by Sex
- **Bulls**: ~700 lbs (318 kg) average; up to 900 lbs (408 kg)
- **Cows**: ~500 lbs (227 kg) average

### Bull Weight Loss During Rut
- **20% of body weight** lost during September-October rut
- For a 700 lb (318 kg) bull: ~140 lbs (64 kg) loss
- Can prove fatal for unfit bulls entering winter

### Calving Dates and Calf Birth Weight
- Gestation: ~250 days (8 months)
- Calving: **late May to June**
- Calf birth weight: **30-40 lbs (14-18 kg)**; singles heavier than twins
- Calves stand and nurse within 30 minutes of birth
- Weight gain: >1 lb/day in first months

### Migration Distances and Timing
- Average migration distance: **39 miles (63 km)**; farthest documented: **168 miles (270 km)**
- Northern herd: 10-140 km to at least 12 summer areas
- Spring migration: triggered by snowmelt and vegetation green-up; delayed after heavy snowpack
- Fall migration: timing varies annually but elk leave summer ranges at roughly the same time regardless of elevation or wolf numbers
- 414 elk across 9 herds GPS-collared between 2001-2017

### Herd Size
- Northern Yellowstone Herd winter count: **5,000-8,000** animals
- ~6-7 herds summer in Yellowstone; largest is the Northern herd

### Seasonal Elevation Use
- Winter: lower elevations on the Northern Range and valleys outside the park
- Summer: high plateaus across nearly all 2.2M acres of YNP
- Yellowstone Northern Range: predominantly 1,500-2,400 m
- Specific meters by season: **Not precisely quantified** in search results

### Daily Movement Distance
- Strong crepuscular activity pattern
- Specific daily km values: **Not found** as a clean number; movement tied to forage tracking ("surfing the green wave")

### Mortality Rates by Cause and Season
- **Source**: Evans et al. 2006, J. Wildlife Management (adult female elk post-wolf restoration)
- Annual mortality rates for adult females:
  - All predators including wolves: **0.09** (95% CI: 0.05-0.14)
  - Hunting: **0.08** (CI: 0.04-0.13)
  - Wolves specifically: **0.07** (CI: 0.03-0.11)
- Seasonal pattern: mortality highest in **January-February**
- Calf mortality shifts post-wolves: pre-wolf 93% of juvenile deaths from starvation; post-wolf only 17% from starvation, with predation becoming dominant
- Juvenile wolf predation rate: ~4% in multi-population study

**Key Sources:**
- [NPS - Elk Yellowstone](https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/nature/elk.htm)
- [Evans et al. 2006 - Survival of Adult Female Elk](https://wildlife.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2193/0022-541X(2006)70%5B1372:SOAFEI%5D2.0.CO;2)
- [White et al. 2010 - Migration of Northern Yellowstone Elk](https://academic.oup.com/jmammal/article/91/4/827/966635)
- [Berkeley News - Yellowstone Elk Migrations](https://news.berkeley.edu/2019/06/14/what-drives-yellowstones-massive-elk-migrations/)
- [Elk Calf Survival and Mortality Following Wolf Restoration](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1099&context=usgsnpwrc)

## 3. Grizzly Bear (Ursus arctos) -- Greater Yellowstone

### Weight by Sex
- **Males (boars)**: 300-700 lbs (136-318 kg); average ~400-500 lbs
- **Females (sows)**: 200-400 lbs (91-181 kg)
- Pre-hibernation (peak): heaviest in October-November after hyperphagia
- Post-hibernation (spring emergence): lightest

### Hibernation Dates
- **Den entry**:
  - Females: begins 4th week of September; 90% denned by 4th week of November
  - Males: den later than females
- **Emergence**:
  - Males: mid-to-late March; 90% emerged by 4th week of April
  - Females without cubs: earliest 3rd week of March; 90% by 1st week of May
  - Females with cubs: emerge latest, April to early May
- **Denning duration**:
  - Females with cubs: average **171 days**
  - Other females: average **151 days**
  - Males: average **131 days**

### Weight Loss During Hibernation
- **15-30% of total body weight** (conservative estimate)
- 20-40% of fat reserves specifically
- No muscle mass lost during hibernation

### Hyperphagia Weight Gain Rate
- **Up to 1.65 kg/day (3.64 lbs/day)**
- Consuming up to 20,000 calories/day
- Period: August through November (2-4 months before denning)

### Home Range Size by Sex
- **Females**: average **884 km2**
- **Males**: average **3,757 km2**
- Males consistently larger ranges than females
- Female home range more strongly associated with population density than food availability

### Daily Movement Distance
- Mean daily activity radius: **1.5 km** (used as grid cell size in distribution studies)
- Males: up to 2,000 miles over an active season (criss-crossing territory)
- Over a full summer: >1,000 miles total
- Specific daily km by season (spring vs summer vs fall): **Not precisely separated** in search results

### Mortality Causes and Rates
- Human development is the strongest predictor of survival
- Survival improves with secure habitat and elevation; declines with road density, homes, and developments
- Bears in areas open to fall ungulate hunting: higher mortality
- Specific cause percentages: **Not broken out** in search results

### Cub Production and Survival
- Litter size: most commonly **2 cubs (61%)**, then 3 (21%), then 1 (18%)
- Cub birth: **January-February** (in the den)
- Annual cub survival: ~**55%** in Yellowstone; ~65% in Cabinet-Yaak
- Population growth: slowed from 4.2-7.6%/year (1983-2001) to 0.3-2.2%/year (2002-2011)
- IGBST has radio-monitored >830 individuals since 1975

## 4. Mountain Lion / Cougar (Puma concolor)

### Weight by Sex
- **Males**: 115-220 lbs (52-100 kg)
- **Females**: 64-141 lbs (29-64 kg)

### Home Range / Territory Size by Sex
- **Males**: 100-250 sq mi (259-648 km2); average ~150 sq mi (388 km2)
- **Females**: 30-100 sq mi (78-259 km2); average ~50 sq mi (130 km2)

### Daily Movement Distance
- Average: ~2.3 miles/day (3.7 km/day) (West Texas study)
- Can cover up to 20 miles (32 km) in a single night
- Spring/summer: up to 20-30 miles/day when establishing/defending territories

### Kill Rate
- Females raising cubs: **every 3-5 days** (1 deer)
- Adult males: **every 5-14 days**
- General estimate: **1-2 deer per week**
- Guard and feed on carcasses for several days

### Kitten Production
- Litter size: **1-6 cubs; typically 2-4**, mode of 2
- Gestation: 90-96 days
- Birthing season: year-round but peaks vary by latitude
- Juveniles stay with mother: **12-24 months**
- Kitten survival: **60-66%** in unhunted populations (Logan & Sweanor, New Mexico)
- Dispersal distance: up to 300 air miles from natal area

### Mortality Rates and Causes
- Annual mortality rate (unhunted population, southern Utah): **26%**
- Greatest overall cause: **human-related** (poaching, vehicle collisions, sport/depredation hunting)
- Florida panther: ~50% of documented mortality from vehicle collisions (1979-1991)
- Old age: extreme tooth wear leads to starvation

### Activity Patterns
- Primarily **crepuscular to nocturnal** (dusk to dawn)
- Shifts to more nocturnal in areas with high human activity
- In protected areas: can be cathemeral (active throughout 24h cycle)
- Activity pattern correlated with presence of other predators and prey

**Key Sources:**
- [Mountain Lion Foundation - About Mountain Lions](https://mountainlion.org/about-mountain-lions/)
- [NWF - Mountain Lion](https://www.nwf.org/Educational-Resources/Wildlife-Guide/Mammals/Mountain-Lion)
- [Wikipedia - Cougar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cougar)
- [Borderlands Research Institute - Mountain Lion Movements](https://bri.sulross.edu/conservation-biology/mountain-lion-movements-and-dispersal/)
- [Logan & Sweanor - New Mexico cougar research](https://cougarfund.org/about-the-cougar/family-life/)

## 5. Mule Deer (Odocoileus hemionus)

### Weight by Sex
- **Males (bucks)**: average **160 lbs (73 kg)**
- **Females (does)**: average **130 lbs (59 kg)**
- Winter weight loss: **20-25%** of body weight

### Migration Distance and Timing
- Average: varies widely by herd
  - Sheep Mountain herd (WY): average **20 miles (32 km)** one-way; some >50 miles (80 km)
  - DuBois herd: longest **105 miles (169 km)** ending in YNP
  - Red Desert to Hoback corridor: **240 km (150 miles)** one-way -- longest mule deer corridor in the world
  - Record: Deer 255 -- **242 miles (390 km)** one-way, ~480-mile round trip (longest in lower 48)
- Fall trigger: persistent snow >12 inches deep triggers movement to lower elevations
- Timing: climate, plant phenology, and life-history dependent

### Fawning Dates and Fawn Birth Weight
- Breeding: mid-November to mid-December
- Gestation: ~203 days (range: 173-233)
- Fawning: **late May to June**
- Birth weight: **5-11 lbs (2.3-5 kg)**; singles weigh more than multiples; one source says average ~8 lbs (3.6 kg)

### Daily Movement by Season
- Males during rut: ~7.4 km/day (white-tailed deer comparative data)
- Males post-rut: ~6.2 km/day
- Females: 2.7-3.4 km/day (varies by reproductive state)
- Strong crepuscular pattern; most pronounced in spring and autumn
- Mule deer-specific daily distance: **Not cleanly separated** from white-tailed deer in search results

### Herd Size
- Variable; winter aggregations can be large
- GPS studies have collared 210+ deer in single studies (30 males, 80 females, 100 fawns)

### Mortality Rates by Cause and Season
- Annual female survival: **0.55-0.90** (mean 0.75 across herds and years)
- Known mortality causes:
  - Mountain lion predation: **69%** of known deaths (one study)
  - Vehicle collision: **19%**
  - CWD + predation dominant in Wyoming: 32/91 deaths from CWD, 27/91 from predation
- Greatest mortality: **winter and spring**
- Positively related to temperature anomalies in those seasons

**Key Sources:**
- [USGS - Home for the Holidays: Deer 255's 242-mile Journey](https://www.usgs.gov/news/featured-story/home-holidays-follow-one-mule-deers-242-mile-journey-across-changing-american)
- [Red Desert to Hoback Corridor - Conservation Corridor](https://conservationcorridor.org/ccsg/what-we-do/projects-and-activities/guidelines/case-studies/wyoming/)

## 6. Moose (Alces alces)

### Weight by Sex and Season
- **Males (bulls)**: 1,200-1,600 lbs (542-725 kg) in prime condition
- **Females (cows)**: 800-1,300 lbs (364-591 kg)
- Shiras moose (Rocky Mountain subspecies) are the smallest subspecies -- on the lower end of these ranges
- Monthly breakdown: **Not found** in search results

### Home Range Size
- Females: average **~15 sq mi (39 km2)** dormant season; increases by 1.5-7 sq mi during growth season
- Males: average **~38 sq mi (98 km2)** dormant season
- Heavy snow winters: range compressed to **1.5 km2 (0.6 sq mi)**
- Low-snow winters: **7 km2 (3 sq mi)**
- Moose are generally solitary

### Calving Dates and Calf Birth Weight
- Gestation: ~230 days
- Calving: **mid-May to early June**
- Calf birth weight: **28-35 lbs (13-16 kg)**; rarely up to 45 lbs (20 kg)
- Peak lactation: ~21 days post-birth

### Daily Movement by Season
- Seasonal movements: **a few miles to 60 miles** (5-97 km) between calving, rutting, and wintering areas
- Daily movement values: **Not quantified** in search results

### Mortality Rates
- Wyoming adult cow survival: **62-91%** across years (1999-2002)
- Calf survival: **73%** through first 2 months; **50%** through winter (Jackson area)
- Main causes: wolf predation, bacterial infection from predator injuries, parasites (liver flukes, brain worms, winter ticks)
- Winter tick (Dermacentor albipictus) and arterial worm (Elaeophora schneideri): population-level impacts

**Key Sources:**

## 7. Pronghorn (Antilocapra americana)

### Weight by Sex
- **Males**: 88-143 lbs (40-65 kg)
- **Females**: 75-106 lbs (34-48 kg)

### Migration Distance
- **Path of the Pronghorn**: ~**150 miles (241 km)** one-way between Grand Teton NP summer range and Pinedale, WY winter range
- Full round-trip: **~200 miles (322 km)** -- longest land migration in lower 48
- 300-400 pronghorn make this migration each fall/spring
- Yellowstone pronghorn: partially migratory; >70% migrate **15-50 km** to 4 contiguous summer areas; <30% are year-round residents
- **Source**: 5,743 telemetry locations of 44 adult females, June 1999-August 2005

### Fawning Dates
- **Late May to early June**
- Usually **twins**
- Fawn birth weight: **5-7 lbs (2.3-3.2 kg)**

### Herd Size by Season
- Variable: **3 to 1,000 individuals**
- Typical groups: **20 to hundreds**
- Larger aggregations in winter; smaller bachelor groups and nursery bands in summer

### Daily Movement and Speed
- Top speed: **55 mph (88.5 km/h)** for ~800 m
- Sustained speeds: 42 mph (68 km/h) for 1.5 km; 35 mph (56 km/h) for 6.5 km
- Fastest land mammal in North America
- Daily movement distances: **Not quantified** as a clean number in search results

### Seasonal Elevation Use
- Recent research challenges conventional elevation limits
- Documented using **high-elevation summer ranges** in Carter Mountain area within GYE
- Traditionally considered low-elevation/sagebrush specialists
- Specific elevation numbers: **Not found** in search results

**Key Sources:**

- [Conservation Fund - Path of the Pronghorn](https://www.conservationfund.org/our-impact/projects/the-path-of-the-pronghorn-in-wyoming/)
- [Wyoming Game & Fish - Pronghorn Species Profile](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/wyoming-wildlife/wyoming-wildlife-magazine/american-pronghorn-species-profile)

## 8. Bighorn Sheep (Ovis canadensis)

### Weight by Sex
- **Rams**: 160-250 lbs (73-113 kg); occasionally up to 300+ lbs (136+ kg)
- Some Alberta populations: rams up to **500 lbs (227 kg)** at rut peak
- **Ewes**: 117-200 lbs (53-91 kg)

### Seasonal Altitudinal Migration
- Summer: upland/alpine areas
- Winter: sheltered lower valleys
- Return to the same lambing areas year after year
- Specific elevation numbers: **Not quantified** in search results

### Lambing Dates
- Rocky Mountain bighorn: **April-June** (concentrated)
- Desert bighorn: January-June (majority February-April)
- Gestation: ~6 months

### Band Size
- Typically **8-10** individuals
- Can form herds of **100+**
- Segregated bands: ewe/lamb groups separate from ram groups outside rut

### Ram Weight Loss During Rut
- Rut timing (Rocky Mountain): **October-January**, peak in early December
- Energy expenditure leaves rams in poor winter condition
- Higher mortality among dominant rams after rut
- Specific percentage of weight loss: **Not quantified** in search results (this is a genuine gap -- the data for ungulate rut weight loss is much better documented for elk than for bighorn)

### Disease Vulnerability (Pneumonia)
- **Pathogen**: Mycoplasma ovipneumoniae + secondary bacterial pathogens
- Initial all-age outbreak mortality: **median ~47%**; range **5-100%**
- Alberta data: 7 outbreaks since 1937, mortality **10-75%**
- After initial outbreak: disease persists for **years to decades**, largely restricted to lambs
- Chronic carrier dams transmit to lambs in nursery groups
- Result: impaired recruitment and slow/prevented population recovery
- Removal of chronic carrier ewes can eliminate pneumonia from a population

## Honest Assessment of Gaps

The following parameters could **not** be found as clean published numbers through web search and would require directly accessing PDFs of the Yellowstone Wolf Project Annual Reports, IGBST Annual Reports, or specific journal articles:

1. **Monthly weight curves** for any species -- nobody publishes month-by-month weight for wild populations (captive data exists but is not representative)
2. **Wolf daily movement by season** -- the ~16-32 km/day figure is an overall average; winter vs summer breakdown needs Mech or Smith's primary data
3. **Elk daily movement distance** -- well-studied via GPS but not reduced to a single published "km/day" number in the sources I could access
4. **Elk seasonal elevation in meters** -- Northern Range is ~1,500-2,400 m; summer interior is higher, but specific herd-level means need GIS analysis papers
5. **Bighorn ram rut weight loss %** -- surprisingly poorly documented compared to elk
6. **Grizzly daily movement by season** -- the 1.5 km daily activity radius seems low and likely refers to a different metric; total daily distance needs primary GPS data
7. **Pronghorn daily movement distance** -- well-collared species but summary stats not found
8. **Moose daily movement distance** -- same gap

For a simulation, you would need to access:
- The **Yellowstone Wolf Project Annual Reports** (PDFs from NPS, freely available) for wolf-specific vital rates
- **IGBST Annual Reports** for grizzly bear vital rates
- The **Wyoming Migration Initiative** datasets for ungulate GPS tracks
- **Kauffman et al.** and **Sawyer et al.** papers for mule deer/elk/pronghorn movement parameters
