\section{Mechanism: What LoRA Changes}
\label{sec:mechanism}
% ============================================================

The recovery in \S\ref{sec:recoveries:lora} shows the capability is
present in the pretrained architecture; \S\ref{sec:dynamics} shows it
remains unsurfaced through every documented training stage. We now
ask what changes inside the model when an 18{,}000-example LoRA
surfaces it. Four mechanistic methods --- probing, logit lens,
attention routing, and causal ablation --- converge on the same
answer: the pretrained model already represents
$v_{\text{last}}$ internally and has an intact readout, but the
partial circuit between them is too weak and lacks downstream
redundancy. LoRA amplifies that partial circuit in 15 attention heads
of layers 30--33 and installs new downstream redundancy at L33+.
All measurements use Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct on Arbitrary-Single at
$K{=}2, N{=}5$ unless noted otherwise (layer indexing 0-based, L0--L35);
Table~\ref{tab:mechanism} reports bootstrap 95\% CIs on the focal deltas
and Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism} shows the per-layer trajectories.

\subsection{The substrate already exists in baseline}
\label{sec:mechanism:substrate}

A linear probe trained on the residual stream to distinguish the FVQ
from the CVQ prompt reaches 100\% accuracy from layer~6 onward in
\emph{both} the baseline and LoRA models; LoRA adds nothing to this
signal. The pretrained model already knows which question it is being
asked. A second probe, trained to predict whether the model
\emph{will get the FVQ right}, reaches 81\% accuracy at L33 in baseline ---
the model also internally tracks FVQ correctness.

What the baseline does \emph{not} encode is whether it will get the
CVQ right. The CVQ-correctness probe at L27--L33 sits at chance in
baseline (53--58\%) while the corresponding FVQ probe is at 77--81\%.
Logit lens reveals the parallel pattern at the token level: at L32 of
the baseline, $\Pr(v_{\text{last}}){=}0.34$ --- the answer surfaces
briefly --- then decays to $\Pr(v_{\text{last}}){=}0.25$ at L35.
\emph{Found then suppressed.} The pretrained model identifies the
query, partially computes the answer, and has an intact readout in
layers 34--35 (their attention patterns are nearly identical pre- and
post-LoRA; Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}b); it fails only because the
partial answer does not propagate.

\subsection{LoRA amplifies a partial v\textsubscript{last} promoter
circuit in layers 30--33}
\label{sec:mechanism:promoter}

Attention routing locates the change precisely. We measure, per head,
the probability of attending from the generation token to the round
containing $v_{\text{last}}$ under the CVQ prompt. Fifteen heads in the
L30--L33 band (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}b) gain substantial
$v_{\text{last}}$ attention: pre-LoRA values of 0.05--0.23 rise to
0.60--0.86 post-LoRA --- a 4--12$\times$ increase per head (specific
heads listed in Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism}). Layers 0--20 and 34--35
attention patterns are essentially unchanged (small negative shifts
under $-0.06$); the rewiring is spatially confined to a four-layer
band in the upper-middle of the stack.

The pre-LoRA values are non-zero. These heads were already
\emph{partial} $v_{\text{last}}$ attenders before any LoRA training,
and (\S\ref{sec:mechanism:causal}) they already carried causal
$v_{\text{last}}$ signal in baseline. LoRA does not install them;
it amplifies a partial pre-existing circuit. The token-level
consequence is immediate: baseline L32 has
$\Pr(v_{\text{last}}){=}0.34$, post-LoRA L32 has 0.99 at
$K{=}2, N{=}5$ and 0.62 at the harder $K{=}2, N{=}50$ cell, and
$\Pr(v_{\text{last}})$ saturates to 1.0 by L33--L35 even at the
harder cell. At the representational level the CVQ-correctness probe
rises from chance to 92--94\% at L27--L33 (bootstrap 95\% CI
$\Delta {=} +0.36~[+0.21, +0.50]$ at L33), installed in the same
late layers that already carried the FVQ-correctness signal.

A naive alternative would be that LoRA \emph{removes} the late-layer
suppression that attenuated $v_{\text{last}}$ in baseline. Stage~3
causal ablation rules this out. Ablating the five baseline suppressor
heads (L26H3, L27H3, L29H3, L29H4, L30H3) in the LoRA model still
produces a measurable release of $v_{\text{last}}$ at L31
($\Delta{=}{+}0.150$, \emph{larger} than the $+0.078$ release seen
when the same heads are ablated in baseline). The suppressors keep
firing post-LoRA with more signal to suppress; LoRA strengthens a
parallel promoter pathway that overpowers them.

\subsection{Two LoRA changes: amplification and downstream
redundancy}
\label{sec:mechanism:causal}

To distinguish what LoRA \emph{installs} from what it \emph{amplifies}, we
ablate the eight highest-attribution promoter heads in both the
baseline and the LoRA model and compare the resulting trajectories.
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_fourway} summarises the four conditions;
within-run paired $\Delta$ at L32 is the trustworthy statistic
(trial seeds are not pinned across runs).

Two findings emerge. First, ablating the eight heads in the
\emph{baseline} model drops L32 $\Pr(v_{\text{last}})$ by $-0.38$,
\emph{comparable in magnitude to the $-0.28$ drop in the LoRA model}.
The heads are not LoRA-installed; they already carry partial causal
$v_{\text{last}}$ signal pre-training (consistent with their non-zero
pre-LoRA $\Pr(\text{attend})$), and LoRA's contribution is best
described as \emph{amplification} of a partially functional circuit.

Second, the downstream propagation of the perturbation differs
sharply between baseline and LoRA. In baseline, ablating the L30--L33
promoters propagates through readout: L33 $\Delta {=} -0.13$, L35
$\Delta {=} -0.09$. In the LoRA model, the same ablation is absorbed
by L33+: L33 $\Delta {=} {+}0.00$, L35 $\Delta {=} {+}0.00$. The
downstream layers have gained compensatory pathways that re-amplify
$v_{\text{last}}$ when the upstream signal is partially knocked out.
This redundancy is genuinely new --- it does not pre-exist in the
baseline architecture in any form we can detect by ablation.

\paragraph{Synthesis.}
The four methods form a closed causal chain: attention heads in
L30--L33 attend to $v_{\text{last}}$ (attention routing) $\to$
$v_{\text{last}}$ builds in the residual stream at L32 (logit lens)
$\to$ a CVQ-correctness feature becomes linearly decodable at L27--L33
(probing) $\to$ behaviour recovers (\S\ref{sec:recoveries:lora}).
The substrate was therefore even more complete than a first reading
suggests: the $v_{\text{last}}$ representation, condition
discrimination, intact readout, \emph{and} a partial promoter circuit
were all present pre-training. The pretraining failure is a gain and
robustness failure --- the partial circuit fires too weakly to win
the tug-of-war against suppression, and the readout has no redundancy
to compensate when the upstream signal is incomplete. 18\,000 LoRA
examples turn up the gain and install error-correcting pathways. We
discuss what this implies for the broader latent capability claim in
\S\ref{sec:discussion}.
