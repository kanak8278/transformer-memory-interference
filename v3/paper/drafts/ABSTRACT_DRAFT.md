# Abstract Drafts

## Draft 1 (Empirical focus, corrected claims)

When autoregressive language models process sequences containing repeated key-value updates, they reliably retrieve the first value (retroactive interference resistance, RI) but systematically fail at retrieving the last value (proactive interference resistance, PI). We present a cross-architecture mechanistic investigation of this PI > RI asymmetry across 9 models spanning 5 transformer variants and the Mamba state-space model. Using logit lens analysis, we show that the correct last value IS computed at intermediate layers but gets outcompeted by adjacent values in the final layers. Probing classifiers reveal that RI success is well-encoded in the residual stream (87% probe accuracy) while PI success is only weakly above chance (61%). Crucially, the Mamba SSM — which lacks causal attention, softmax, and positional encoding — shows the same PI > RI pattern (gap = +57%), eliminating these as sole causes. Jacobian analysis at initialization reveals that the primacy bias is architectural: present in random-weight models (first-quarter influence 1.47x middle for transformers, extreme primacy for SSMs). We formalize two conditions — monotone overwrite and diminishing marginal overwrite — prove the first for single-layer attention, derive the second from existing theory (Chowdhury, 2026), and show they predict all observed phenomena. PI accuracy decays exponentially with the number of updates while RI remains robust. Our results establish that PI > RI is a pervasive property of autoregressive sequence processing, not a fixable bug in a few attention heads.

## Draft 2 (Theory + empirical, corrected)

Autoregressive language models exhibit a systematic retrieval asymmetry: given a sequence of key-value updates, they reliably recall the first value (RI) but fail at the last (PI). We provide cross-architecture mechanistic evidence explaining why. Across 9 models spanning transformer variants and a state-space model, we find PI > RI in 91% of conditions (mean gap: 50 percentage points). We show this asymmetry is architectural, not learned: Jacobian analysis at initialization reveals primacy bias in untrained models of both architecture classes. Logit lens analysis shows the last value is found at intermediate layers but outcompeted in the final layers, while first-value retrieval is clean and monotonic. Probing classifiers demonstrate the asymmetry exists in representation space: RI correctness is well-encoded (87%) but PI correctness is only weakly above chance (61%). Our cross-architecture comparison eliminates causal attention, softmax, and RoPE as sole causes, narrowing the candidate mechanisms to autoregressive processing, continuous gating, and fixed-capacity state. We formalize two empirically-verifiable conditions under which first-value retrieval necessarily dominates last-value retrieval, prove the key lemma for single-layer attention, and verify all conditions hold in 4 models via logit lens trajectories. These findings demonstrate that PI > RI is a pervasive property of sequential processing across architectures.

## Draft 3 (Shortest, corrected)

Language models remember first and forget last. We show this is architectural. Across 9 models including Mamba SSMs (no attention), 91% of test conditions show first-value retrieval dominating last-value retrieval by 50 percentage points. The asymmetry exists at initialization (Jacobian analysis), in representations (probing: 87% vs 61% correctness encoding), and in intermediate computations (logit lens: correct value found then suppressed). We eliminate causal attention, softmax, and positional encoding as sole causes, narrowing to autoregressive processing with continuous gating. We formalize two conditions (monotone overwrite, diminishing marginal overwrite), prove the first for single-layer attention, derive the second from existing theory, and verify both empirically. PI accuracy decays exponentially with update count while RI remains robust.
